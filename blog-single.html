The Hidden Liabilities in Standard Commercial Agreements: What Founders Overlook

Most standard boilerplate clauses are drafted to protect someone else’s enterprise. Here is how strategic corporate counsel audits agreements before costly disputes arise.

By Michelle Bomberger, Managing Partner • September 18, 2026 • 6 min read
Home » Home » Blog & Insights » Hidden Liabilities in Commercial Contracts

Every business relationship is founded on an agreement. Whether signing a software vendor contract, bringing on a strategic partner, or finalizing a major client engagement, entrepreneurs often accept standard form agreements under the assumption that boilerplate language is neutral.

In practice, boilerplate language is rarely neutral. More often than not, it was written by the opposing party’s counsel to shift risk, restrict remedies, and tilt potential dispute resolution heavily in their own favor. When businesses experience legal friction, it is rarely over the basic scope of work—it is over the fine-print provisions that were overlooked during execution.

“A commercial contract is not just a mechanism for when deals go right. It is your business’s insurance policy and operational playbook for when expectations collide.”

Michelle Bomberger • Managing Partner, Equinox Business Law Group

1. The Boilerplate Illusion

Entrepreneurs are built for speed. When a customer demands quick onboarding or a key supplier issues a 22-page master services agreement (MSA), leadership naturally focuses on commercial pricing, delivery deadlines, and payment terms.

However, the provisions buried toward the back of the contract—indemnification, limitation of liability, intellectual property assignments, and governing law—often carry far greater financial exposure than the total value of the transaction itself.

2. Unilateral Indemnity Triggers

One of the most dangerous terms in commercial contracting is the unilateral indemnity clause. In many vendor templates, one party promises to defend, indemnify, and hold harmless the other from any and all claims, liabilities, damages, or costs arising from the agreement.

If your business signs a one-sided indemnity clause, you could find yourself funding the other party’s legal defense even if their own negligence contributed to the problem. Strategic counsel routinely audits this language to ensure mutual obligations and reasonable scoping.

Standard Boilerplate Trap Equinox Recommended Language
One-Sided Indemnity:
“Company shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless Vendor from any and all claims, costs, or losses arising out of or related to this Agreement.” 		Mutual, Scoped Indemnity:
“Each party shall indemnify the other solely against third-party claims arising directly from gross negligence, willful misconduct, or material breach of confidentiality.”
Silent or Uncapped Liability:
No limitation of liability, exposing your business’s entire balance sheet to consequential damages or lost profits. 		Balanced Cap & Exclusion:
Cap total aggregate liability to the fees paid under the contract in the preceding 12 months, with mutual waiver of indirect or consequential damages.

3. Ambiguous Automatic Renewal Traps

Evergreen clauses and automatic renewal terms are designed to lock businesses into recurring commitments unless notice is delivered within narrow, easily missed windows (such as “no later than 90 days prior to the expiration date via certified mail”).

Without centralized contract tracking, companies regularly find themselves locked into six-figure renewals for redundant software licenses or legacy vendor agreements that no longer align with company strategy.

4. Governing Law & Dispute Venues

If your company operates in Seattle, Washington, agreeing to litigate disputes in New York, Delaware, or England imposes an immediate financial penalty should conflict arise. The expense of retaining local counsel, traveling for depositions, and filing out-of-state pleadings often forces growing businesses into unfavorable settlements.

Equinox counsel always evaluates dispute resolution mechanisms, advocating for local jurisdiction, mutual mediation steps, and reciprocal prevailing-party attorney fee clauses to prevent frivolous bullying.

5. The Proactive Contract Audit Checklist

Before executing any material agreement, every executive team should run through this five-point diagnostic:

  • Scope of Representations: Are the performance warranties realistic, or are you promising perfection?
  • IP Ownership: Does the agreement clearly state that your pre-existing intellectual property remains solely yours?
  • Termination for Convenience: Can your company exit if circumstances change, or are you trapped without cause?
  • Data Security & Compliance: Does the vendor have contractual duties to protect your proprietary client data?
  • Audit Rights: Is the other party permitted to inspect your books and operations without reasonable notice?

By engaging experienced corporate counsel to review contracts through a fixed-fee general counsel relationship, your business protects what it has built while moving forward with speed and confidence.
